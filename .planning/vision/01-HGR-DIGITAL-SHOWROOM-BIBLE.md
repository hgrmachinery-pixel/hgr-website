## Why HGR Exists

HGR's website gives customers a clear view of real used excavators and a practical route to speak with HGR. Machinery decisions involve substantial cost, operational consequence, and physical inspection. The website supports that decision by making known information easy to find and by making direct contact straightforward.

The website is not a transaction engine. It does not replace a conversation, a quotation, a machine inspection, or an agreed viewing. Other business areas may be represented in proportion to their verified current scope; their presence must not distract from the principal used-excavator business.

## The HGR Standard

The HGR Standard is the quality of the information and experience, not a promotional claim. Public content must be accurate, specific to the machine or service described, and maintained when circumstances change. Unknown information is omitted or clearly identified as unavailable; it is never filled with an assumption.

A machine's identity, publication state, and commercial availability are distinct. Available, Reserved, and Sold describe the machine's status. Draft and Published describe whether its page is public. One physical unit always remains one record, with a stable identity and URL through changes in status.

The site should be calm, legible, and practical. It should help a customer compare the information HGR has, understand what remains to ask, and choose a direct next step.

## Customer Psychology

A prospective buyer may arrive with a specific model in mind, or may be learning what is available. They may be comparing machines remotely before committing time to a viewing. They need confidence that the page describes a real unit and that contacting HGR will refer to that same unit.

The experience should reduce uncertainty without pretending to eliminate it. Clear machine identity, visible status, known specifications, useful photographs, and direct contact reduce avoidable effort. The design should not manufacture urgency, imply guarantees, obscure missing information, or present an image as proof of a fact it cannot establish.

Customers may use a phone, have limited bandwidth, or rely on keyboard and assistive technology. Core content and actions must remain understandable across these conditions.

## The Memory

The showroom remembers individual physical machines. Stable identifiers and URLs distinguish units even when brand, model, and year match. A sold machine page remains a truthful record and is not recycled for a later unit. Its status and next step change, while its identity does not.

This memory serves customers who revisit a listing, follow a shared link, or encounter it through search. It also gives HGR a coherent record of what was presented. Retention and public visibility are separate matters: private fields, sensitive documents, drafts, and internal data remain protected.

## Customer Journey

The intended journey is direct:

1. Understand that HGR offers used excavators.
2. Browse currently available machines or reach the wider inventory.
3. Open one physical machine's page and inspect its details and photographs.
4. Ask a question by an available contact route, referring to that machine.
5. Discuss availability and arrange an in-person viewing where appropriate.

Reserved and sold units are labelled truthfully and guide customers toward an appropriate enquiry or available stock. Supporting pages for rental, machinery services, lubricants, about, and contact should remain proportionate and factual. Viewing is arranged directly, preferably by appointment; location and individual machine location claims require verification.

## Digital Yard Philosophy

The site is a digital yard: an organised way to inspect the machines HGR chooses to present. Like a physical yard, it should make orientation easy, preserve a sense of the individual unit, and help a visitor decide what to examine more closely.

This metaphor guides hierarchy and content, not a literal simulation. The site does not need artificial yard maps, virtual tours, or other features outside the agreed scope. The digital yard is useful when it makes genuine inventory easier to understand and act on.

## Living Machine

A machine page represents a real physical unit whose circumstances can change. Its facts, photographs, price presentation, publication state, and availability are maintained as the unit changes from available to reserved or sold.

The page must not confuse a changing commercial status with a changing identity. Updates should be timely and visible, while historical pages remain accurate about the status they communicate. A sold unit can continue to be useful as a record and as a path to other available machines.

## Trust Through Evidence

Trust is earned through accurate detail, clear status, and evidence presented with restraint. Specifications are entered or verified by HGR. Year of manufacture and registration date are separate facts. Machine origin or registration status is stated only when explicitly verified. No claim such as “Recond” or “Local New” is inferred from a document alone.

Photography can show condition and components, but should not be framed as proof beyond what is visible. A specification may link to relevant evidence, such as an hour-meter photograph, when that relationship is clear. Serial number visibility is an independent decision. Sensitive registration and ownership documents require private originals; a distinct redacted copy requires human review and explicit approval before public use.

## Photography Philosophy

Machine photography is a structured record, not a pile of uploads. General/exterior, side, front, rear, cabin, hour meter, chassis/serial, engine, undercarriage, boom/arm/bucket, and other-detail views provide an organising vocabulary. Not every category is required for every unit. A suitable featured/general image is required before a machine is publicly presented.

The public experience brings categories together in one coherent gallery with useful navigation. This avoids forcing a visitor through a long set of disconnected sections while preserving access to specific views. Owners should be able to choose the featured photograph, order images, preview them, and set a focal point where supported.

High-quality originals should not require manual resizing. The system preserves an appropriate master and creates responsive, optimised delivery sizes. Image filenames derive from stable machine identity and category; factual alt text is maintained separately and does not invent visual details.

## Motion Philosophy

Motion may express the weight and physical character of a real excavator, especially in the homepage hero. The machine body and tracks remain visually anchored while a boom or arm may respond to scroll and reverse where practical.

Motion is visual polish, never a prerequisite for orientation, information, navigation, or contact. It must not delay usable content, impair performance, or create inaccessible interactions. Small screens, weak devices, and reduced-motion preferences receive static or simplified presentation. The transition to available inventory must remain understandable without animation.

## Simplicity

Simplicity means reducing the work required to find and understand a machine, not removing useful evidence. Navigation remains focused, pages use clear hierarchy, and content is concise without hiding material facts. Unknown optional details are omitted rather than filled with empty fields or invented text.

The business owner manages ordinary structured content through the CMS. Controls should match routine tasks and avoid unnecessary technical or layout decisions. On the public side, calls to action should name their purpose and lead to a real, configured route. There is no checkout in V1; enquiries and viewing arrangements remain direct.

## Product Evolution

The first product is English-language and inventory-first. Its foundation supports a changing set of individual machines and the owner's ability to keep them current. Future changes should respond to real inventory, customer behaviour, operating capacity, and verified need.

Other machinery, richer rental or service information, a lubricant catalogue, additional languages, and expanded search experiences may be considered later. Each requires its own evidence and scope decision. The website remains independent of Mudah: a machine link may be shared manually where permitted, but the site does not depend on or synchronise with that marketplace.

## Responsibility

HGR is responsible for the truth and currency of the information it publishes. The product must support this responsibility with understandable editing, clear publication and availability states, and protection for private material. It must not turn missing business facts into public claims.

The engineering foundation is also a responsibility. Content, database, media, and application hosting remain sufficiently decoupled to permit a compatible host change without rewriting the product. Security, accessibility, performance, and customer privacy are baseline requirements, not optional polish.

## North Star

A customer can understand what HGR offers, find a relevant machine, assess the available evidence, and contact HGR to discuss it or arrange a viewing. HGR can keep this information accurate without routine dependence on technical staff.
