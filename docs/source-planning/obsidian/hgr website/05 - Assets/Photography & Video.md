# HGR Photography and Video Standards

## Purpose and sources

Show real machinery clearly and truthfully while keeping the website fast. Sources: [[01 - Brand/Brand Guidelines]], [[01 - Brand/Design System]], [[02 - Website Specification/Admin System]], [[02 - Website Specification/Individual Machine]], [[02 - Website Specification/Homepage]] and [[03 - Content/Content Requirements]].

No image storage, CDN, editing service or animation library is selected here.

## Machine identity and preparation

Associate every photo/video set with the correct unique machine ID. Check model, visible identifiers and actual unit before approval. Preserve original capture files and record the photographer/source and permission.

Photograph in clear light with adequate distance and resolution. Capture useful evidence rather than hiding condition behind dramatic lighting. Do not require unsafe access or operation for a website shot; only qualified people should operate machinery for footage.

## Machinery shot checklist

| View | What it should communicate |
|---|---|
| Front | Whole machine and attachments in context |
| Rear | Counterweight and visible rear condition |
| Left and right sides | Complete profiles, body panels and overall condition |
| Boom and arm | Structure, joints and relevant visible details |
| Bucket | Shape, teeth/edge, attachments and wear |
| Tracks / undercarriage | Track condition and useful visible components |
| Engine area | Clear relevant accessible views without implying an inspection certification |
| Operator cabin | Interior, controls, seat and visible condition |
| Hour meter | Legible reading; do not alter digits or assume it proves total lifetime usage |
| Identification plate | Legible where appropriate and approved for publication; verify exposure of identifiers |
| Defects / condition details | Clear close-ups of known relevant wear, damage or issues |
| Other useful details | Actual attachments/features that help assess this particular unit |

Aim for a useful set, not filler images. The administration target is approximately 10–30 photographs per machine; this is capacity guidance, not a requirement to invent missing views.

A photographed serial/identification plate may expose a Serial Number. Internal storage does not authorise public disclosure: obtain the owner's display choice before publishing the image or transcribing its serial. The serial field is optional and may remain private.

## Featured image and gallery order

Choose a sharp, representative full-machine photograph with the important parts visible. Side or three-quarter views are useful candidates. Use the Design System's 4:3 starting ratio for cards where appropriate, but preserve the full useful view in the gallery.

Keep the featured image consistent across card, homepage, detail and sharing uses. Suggested gallery order: overall views, working components, cabin/engine, meter/plate, condition details. Do not crop away damage to improve a sales impression.

## Permitted corrections

- Exposure and white-balance correction to restore faithful visibility.
- Restrained colour/contrast correction without changing perceived paint or condition.
- Cropping and straightening while retaining meaningful machine details.
- Minor background distraction cleanup that does not conceal material context or alter the machine.
- Modest sharpening without inventing texture or making labels misleading.
- Compression/resizing with visible-detail review.

Preserve an original so an edit can be checked and regenerated.

## AI editing boundaries

Actual inventory photographs must depict the actual machine. Do not replace them with generated machinery or another unit's photographs.

Do not remove defects, repaint panels, change components/attachments, reconstruct false serial numbers, alter hour readings or otherwise improve condition artificially. Background or marketing edits must not imply an unverified location, ownership or capability. If an edit's effect on factual interpretation is uncertain, obtain HGR review before public use [TO CONFIRM].

Creative hero production is a separate marketing use, not a substitute for listing evidence.

## Required upload and processing workflow

1. Owner selects original high-resolution images, preferably in a batch.
2. System shows understandable upload/processing progress and actionable errors.
3. System corrects orientation and generates resized/compressed public versions where appropriate.
4. Generate thumbnail, machinery-card, normal detail and larger gallery sizes, using modern formats where suitable.
5. Target approximately 200 KB or below for generated images where practical while preserving acceptable detail; allow justified larger derivatives.
6. Retain original high-quality unwatermarked copies separately where technically appropriate.
7. Allow featured-image selection, photo ordering and correction/removal of mistaken uploads.
8. Serve responsive sizes; never automatically load every full-resolution original for a card or initial gallery view.
9. Give the first visible machine image appropriate priority and lazy-load later images where appropriate.
10. Verify the output on desktop and common phones before launch.

Physical storage, image service/CDN, limits, job handling and retention are [DEFERRED] for Phase 2. Do not require the owner to manually optimise every image.

Photo categorisation is optional. Do not require the owner to manually classify every photograph before publishing; keep upload, featured selection, ordering/removal and publishing simple.

## Watermarks

The eventual processing system must support:

- No Watermark.
- HGR Logo.
- HGR Logo + hgrmachinery.com.
- Centrally configurable placement, opacity, size and margin.
- Bottom-right as the existing proposed starting position.

Apply watermarking only to generated public copies. Keep source photographs unwatermarked so new derivatives can be generated later. Check small-screen legibility and avoid obscuring identifying details, meters and condition evidence. Exact artwork, opacity, dimensions and default mode are [TO CONFIRM], not new fixed values in this document.

## Video capture and publication

Video is optional. Useful footage includes walkaround, startup, engine running, operation and condition details. Show what the particular machine actually does; do not suggest a comprehensive mechanical inspection unless one has been confirmed.

Keep relevant sequences understandable. Do not use cuts, speed changes, substituted footage or audio replacement to conceal symptoms or exaggerate operation. Use only authorised audio/music and approved identifiable-person footage.

YouTube is the preferred host. Admin should accept a URL and the website should display it without owner-written embed code. Missing video should remove the section automatically. Do not require direct upload of large video files to website hosting.

Use factual titles such as [Brand] [Model] - [actual footage type] - HGR Equipment Resources. Add accurate unit/context details and a relevant machine-page link in the description when available. Do not invent age, hours or condition claims. Existing channel, account access and usable videos are [TO CONFIRM].

Evaluate lightweight previews or interaction-triggered embeds for performance; review privacy behaviour in [[03 - Content/Legal Content]]. Confirm the linked video matches the unit and remains accessible.

## Yard, people, services and lubricant photography

Use real Klang yard imagery and verify that captions do not place every machine there. Owner/team imagery requires permission and confirmed names/roles. Service photography must depict actual confirmed capabilities, not imply a large workshop or emergency operation without evidence.

Lubricant packaging must match actual products. Use HGR photographs or approved manufacturer/supplier assets; never generate fictional packaging or grades.

## Signature homepage excavator assets

Prepare a high-quality side or suitable three-quarter view of a real HGR machine, with clear stationary body/tracks and visible boom, arm and bucket. Obtain supplementary reference views to understand realistic pivots and movement. Starting pose: bucket near/on ground, boom and arm lowered naturally.

Planned motion raises the boom/arm while the body stays anchored, with reverse movement on upward scroll where practical. Capture/source selection should allow natural high-arm presentation without materially altering the machine.

Layered 2D assets, transparent imagery, SVG where appropriate and other lightweight methods remain evaluation options, not selected technology. Layer separation and any reconstruction require review; do not present a simulated movement as evidence of actual operation.

Provide an approved static hero and reduced/mobile fallback. The CTA and inventory access must work immediately. Final asset production method, layer needs and delivery budget are [DEFERRED] until prototyping/performance testing.
